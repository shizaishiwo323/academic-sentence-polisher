# Before/After Examples

These examples show sentence-level polishing only. They should not be used to change manuscript logic, add mechanisms, or strengthen conclusions.

## Example 1: Conservative syntax simplification

Original:
Mineral dissolution widely occurs in natural and engineering processes.

Revised:
Mineral dissolution occurs widely in natural and engineered systems.

Problem:
The original is understandable but slightly awkward.

Why:
The revision keeps the original meaning, uses a simpler verb structure, and avoids adding a new mechanism.

## Example 2: Long abstract subject

Original:
The establishment of a comprehensive structure-signal framework provides new insights into dissolution behavior.

Revised:
This analysis links pore-structure changes to the measured signal during dissolution.

Problem:
The original uses an abstract subject, an invented compound, and a generic contribution phrase.

Why:
The revision states the concrete relation directly without adding a broader claim.

## Example 3: Overclaim

Original:
These results prove that NMR T2 evolution robustly diagnoses dissolution mechanisms in porous media.

Revised:
These results suggest that NMR T2 evolution can indicate dissolution-related pore-structure changes under the tested conditions.

Problem:
`prove` and `robustly diagnoses` are too strong unless direct validation supports them.

Why:
The revision preserves an interpretive claim while limiting scope and evidence strength.

## Example 4: Undefined term

Original:
The pore-coupling index captures the matrix-vug interaction.

Revised:
The pore-coupling index, defined here as the exchange strength between matrix pores and vugs, captures the matrix-vug interaction.

Problem:
The metric is central but undefined.

Why:
The revision adds a local definition. Use this pattern only when the definition is provided or confirmed by the author.

## Example 5: Decorative verb

Original:
The model elucidates the intricate evolution of flow pathways during dissolution.

Revised:
The model shows how flow pathways evolve during dissolution.

Problem:
`elucidates` and `intricate` make the sentence sound more ornate than necessary.

Why:
The revision uses direct verbs and keeps the claim local.

## Example 6: Observation before interpretation

Original:
This mechanism reveals the transition to wormholing as the T2 peak shifts to longer relaxation times.

Revised:
The T2 peak shifts to longer relaxation times as the system transitions to wormholing.

Problem:
The original begins with a broad mechanism claim before reporting the observation.

Why:
The revision reports the observed signal first and avoids adding a stronger mechanism claim.
