# Discussion and Conclusion

## Purpose

Explain what the findings mean, how they refine existing understanding, and where the claims stop.

A Discussion usually widens from specific findings toward broader meaning.

## D1. Revisit the question and synthesize key findings

Begin with the answer or most important finding, not a full repetition of Results.

Synthesize:

- what pattern was found;
- under what conditions;
- which result is central;
- how it answers the original question.

## D2. Map findings to existing research

Show whether findings:

- agree with;
- extend;
- refine;
- qualify;
- contradict;
- reconcile;
- explain;
- fail to reproduce

prior work.

A comparison needs a basis: method, condition, scale, material, population, or theory. Do not call studies inconsistent when they are not comparable.

## D3. Explain contribution and implications

Possible contributions:

- new evidence;
- improved measurement or method;
- extension to a new condition or scale;
- mechanism clarification;
- resolution of disagreement;
- model validation or falsification;
- practical capability.

Derive implications from Results and calibrate mechanism language.

## D4. State limitations, future work, and applications

A useful limitation states:

```text
constraint -> likely effect on interpretation -> bounded consequence
```

Future work should follow from a real limitation or unresolved question. Avoid ritual `more research is needed`.

Applications must be plausible at the demonstrated scale. Separate immediate use from long-term potential.

## Useful logic patterns

```text
key finding -> explanation -> literature comparison -> implication
unexpected finding -> possible explanations -> evidence assessment -> next test
agreement -> extension -> boundary condition
limitation -> effect on claim -> mitigation or future work
```

## Conclusion

A Conclusion may:

- restate the answer at a higher level;
- identify the main contribution;
- state the most defensible implication;
- mention a central limitation or next step.

It should not introduce new evidence, copy the Abstract, repeat every result, or claim universal applicability.

## Common failures

- re-narrating figures instead of interpreting;
- mechanism not prepared or tested;
- literature comparison with no explanation;
- unsupported novelty or importance;
- ritual limitations with no effect on claims;
- unrelated future work;
- applications beyond tested scale;
- claim strength inconsistent with Abstract or Results.
