# Methods / Materials and Methods

## Purpose

Show how the research question was made answerable, reproducible, and credible.

Methods are an evidence chain, not merely an equipment inventory.

## M1. Orient the reader

Possible functions:

- give a short design or workflow overview;
- restate the methodological purpose;
- identify materials, equipment, datasets, sites, or participants;
- provide essential setup information.

Do not repeat the full Introduction.

## M2. Provide reproducible detail and justification

Report as relevant:

- quantities, dimensions, units, concentrations, temperatures, and durations;
- sequence and timing;
- inclusion/exclusion criteria;
- sampling, randomization, and controls;
- boundary and initial conditions;
- instrument settings and calibration;
- preprocessing and quality control;
- equations, algorithms, software versions, and parameters;
- statistical tests and thresholds;
- ethical approval and consent;
- reasons for non-obvious choices.

Give enough detail for replication or informed evaluation.

## M3. Relate the procedure to established methods

Distinguish:

- used as published;
- adapted;
- modified;
- extended;
- newly developed.

State what changed and why when it affects reproducibility or interpretation. Attach citations to the exact borrowed method.

## M4. Report constraints or procedural problems

Mention issues affecting:

- representativeness;
- measurement accuracy;
- missing data;
- instrument limits;
- convergence;
- contamination;
- protocol deviations;
- uncertainty.

State their impact rather than hiding or dramatizing them.

## Tense and voice

Typical defaults:

- study-specific actions: past simple;
- standard procedures, equations, or permanent setup: present simple;
- active voice for analytical decisions and author contributions;
- passive voice when process or object is the focus.

Make the boundary between current and cited work unmistakable.

## Evidence-chain audit

For every major result:

```text
research question
-> design choice
-> measurement or computation
-> quality control
-> analysis
-> uncertainty
```

## Common failures

- actions with no design overview;
- missing parameters, counts, or durations;
- unexplained method choice;
- unclear source of materials or data;
- results mixed into Methods;
- unclear distinction between standard and study-specific procedure;
- passive voice hiding decisions;
- citation to a method without enough local detail;
- software named without versions/settings when they matter.
