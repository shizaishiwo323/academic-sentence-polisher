# Results

## Purpose

Present the evidence needed to answer the research question in an order that lets the reader see and evaluate the pattern.

## R1. Orient

Possible functions:

- briefly revisit the aim or analysis;
- remind the reader of a relevant method;
- give a high-level result sequence;
- state the main pattern before details.

Do not repeat the entire Methods section.

## R2. Present key evidence

A result unit often follows:

```text
result claim -> quantitative or visual evidence -> comparison -> local explanation
```

Use figures and tables as evidence, not as a script to describe every visible value.

A figure callout should identify the relevant panel or variable, state the pattern, and provide decisive numbers or uncertainty where useful.

Interpret important values relative to an explicit reference point.

## R3. Report anomalies and uncertainty

Include:

- null or negative findings;
- outliers or deviations;
- uncertainty and variability;
- model-data mismatch;
- measurement or fit limitations;
- conditions under which the pattern changes.

Do not erase inconvenient evidence. State whether it affects the main interpretation.

## R4. Give a bounded implication

Depending on journal convention, Results may end with a local interpretation or implication.

Keep it close to the evidence. Reserve broad literature integration, general significance, and applications for Discussion.

## Ordering

Choose a principle:

- research question or hypothesis;
- experimental sequence;
- variable or regime;
- spatial or temporal scale;
- primary to secondary outcome;
- observation to mechanism;
- main figures in logical, not merely numerical, order.

## Tense

- past simple for study-specific observations;
- present simple for what a figure shows, equations, or intentionally generalized relationships.

Do not shift tense merely to strengthen a finding.

## Results versus Discussion

Immediate interpretation may be appropriate, especially in combined sections, but:

- evidence must remain distinguishable from explanation;
- literature comparison must not overwhelm the result;
- speculative mechanisms must be signaled;
- broad implications should wait.

## Common failures

- panel-by-panel description without a claim;
- repeating numbers without interpretation;
- `significant` with no statistical meaning;
- changed comparator or denominator;
- hidden null findings;
- methods, results, and broad discussion in one paragraph;
- causal wording from observational evidence;
- order unrelated to the research questions;
- every sentence beginning with `Figure X shows`.

## Paragraph audit

```text
question addressed:
main result:
evidence:
comparison:
uncertainty:
local implication:
```
