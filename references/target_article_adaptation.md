# Target-Article Adaptation

Generic scientific-writing rules are defaults. A well-chosen target corpus reveals field and journal conventions.

## 1. Preferred corpus

Use 3-5 recent research articles that:

- come from the target journal or a close substitute;
- use a similar article type and methodology;
- address a similar scientific audience;
- have clearly identifiable sections;
- are original research articles when the user is writing original research.

Do not infer a whole journal style from one unusual paper.

## 2. Extract patterns, not sentences

Inspect:

- section names and order;
- Introduction length and move sequence;
- Methods tense and voice;
- Results/Discussion separation or integration;
- paragraph length and topic-sentence style;
- figure-callout conventions;
- abstract type, word count, and tense pattern;
- title structure and capitalization;
- reporting verbs and claim-strength patterns.

Learn reusable syntax and rhetorical functions. Do not copy distinctive wording.

## 3. Build a compact journal profile

```text
Journal/article type:
Audience:
Section structure:
Preferred authorial voice:
Methods tense:
Results tense:
Results-Discussion boundary:
Abstract pattern:
Title pattern:
Notable restrictions:
```

## 4. Resolve conflicts

Use this priority:

1. explicit journal instructions;
2. stable pattern across target articles;
3. the user's manuscript terminology;
4. generic rules in this skill.

Do not imitate an error merely because it appears in one paper.

## 5. Report adaptation

Distinguish:

- **required correction:** grammar, ambiguity, evidence error;
- **journal adaptation:** convention supported by the corpus;
- **optional preference:** stylistic variation with no material benefit.

When explaining a corpus pattern, do not claim representativeness from a small sample.
