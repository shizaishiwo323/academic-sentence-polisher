# Dash and Compound Policy

## Core rule

Do not introduce a new hyphenated, en-dash, or em-dash expression merely to make prose sound more academic.

Keep a compound when it is:

- a standard field term;
- a necessary pre-nominal modifier;
- a numerical range;
- an established paired relation;
- already defined consistently in the manuscript.

Rewrite when it is:

- newly invented;
- rhetorically decorative;
- too dense to parse;
- replaceable by clear ordinary syntax;
- a compressed mechanism or causal claim that has not been established.

## Examples of ordinary-syntax repair

- `mechanism-aware interpretation` -> `interpretation based on the proposed mechanism`
- `signal-structure bridge` -> `the relation between the signal and structure`
- `context-sensitive response` -> `a response that depends on context`
- `connectivity-breakthrough nexus` -> `the relation between connectivity and breakthrough`

Use the revised phrase only when its scientific relation is present in the source.

## Standard compounds

Preserve field-standard forms such as `pore-scale`, `time-resolved`, `first-order`, `non-invasive`, and numerical ranges. Do not alter dash type, accents, or LaTeX spelling unless the user requests style normalization.

## Em dashes

Avoid adding em dashes for dramatic emphasis in formal manuscript prose. Prefer commas, parentheses, a colon, or a separate sentence when clearer.

## Output gate

Scan the revision for:

- new `word-word` labels;
- new multipart compounds;
- new terms ending in `-aware`, `-informed`, `-driven`, `-sensitive`, `-based`, or `-linked`;
- em-dash interruptions.

Keep them only when standard, defined, and semantically necessary.
