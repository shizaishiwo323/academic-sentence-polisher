# Abstract and Title

## Abstract purpose

Provide a self-contained compressed representation of the paper.

Obey target-journal word count, headings, and article-type rules before generic guidance.

## Abstract move set

### A1. Context, problem, or gap

Use one or two concise sentences when background is needed. Do not let general context dominate.

### A2. Aim or paper action

State the objective, question, hypothesis, or what the paper does.

### A3. Methods or materials

Identify the design, data, model, experiment, or analysis needed to evaluate the result. Include critical conditions or sample size when they determine relevance.

### A4. Key results, contribution, and bounded implication

Usually the highest-information part:

- main result;
- decisive quantitative values;
- concrete contribution;
- implication at the strength supported by the paper.

Avoid `better performance` without comparator or metric.

### A5. Application, limitation, or future work

Include only when central and allowed by the journal.

## Common patterns

### Expanded or structured

```text
context/problem -> aim -> methods -> results -> conclusion/implication
```

### Compressed

```text
paper action + aim + method
key result + quantitative detail + contribution
bounded implication
```

Methods normally precede Results.

## Abstract tense

- context/problem: present simple;
- methods: past simple;
- study results: past simple;
- current contribution/application: present simple or present perfect;
- present simple for a result only when an intentional generalized relationship is supported.

## Abstract checks

- understandable without the main text;
- abbreviations defined as required;
- main method and result identifiable;
- important search terms present naturally;
- no empty promises such as `will be discussed`;
- numbers and claim strength match the paper;
- citations omitted unless genuinely required;
- word count compliant.

## Title purpose

Predict the paper accurately and make it retrievable.

A strong title is:

- specific about system, process, method, or population;
- concise but not cryptic;
- rich in meaningful search terms;
- neutral in value;
- clear about case-study versus general scope;
- in journal-appropriate capitalization.

Avoid:

- removable openings such as `A study of`;
- overlong noun stacks;
- ambiguous prepositions;
- unexplained abbreviations;
- `novel`, `excellent`, or `reliable` without need;
- modal claims;
- scope broader than the study;
- question titles unless normal for the journal.

## Title workflow

1. State the research question plainly.
2. Identify core variables, relation, system, and scope.
3. Choose a pattern:
   - effect of X on Y under Z;
   - comparison of X and Y for Z;
   - method X for measuring or predicting Y;
   - mechanism or relationship statement;
   - result statement;
   - bounded case-study title.
4. Remove empty words.
5. Check noun-stack and preposition ambiguity.
6. Verify every title claim against the paper.
